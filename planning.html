Planning your presence

Start with your purpose.

A good location supports what you want to do. Prepare a clear brief before discussing a temporary shop, a product launch or a permanent store.

  1. Define the first step

    Are you introducing a brand, selling a collection, testing customer interest or meeting possible partners? These purposes can lead to different choices. Decide what you want to learn or achieve before comparing addresses.

  2. Explore the whole setting

    Look at neighbouring shops, transport and the reasons people visit. A large centre can contain very different retail areas. Ask about the actual space, not only the reputation of the building.

  3. Make the costs comparable

    Compare the same period and type of space. Rent, display construction, staffing, taxes, utilities and deposits are different items. Check what is included. A refundable deposit affects the money needed at the start, even when it is not a final expense.

  4. Check the practical details

    Confirm dates, delivery access, storage, changing facilities and signs. A temporary shop also needs time for setup and removal. Ask which parts of the proposal are firm and which still need approval.

  5. Prepare your next conversation

    A venue will need to understand your products, price range, preferred dates and space requirements. Keep your brief clear enough to explain in a short conversation, while leaving room to discuss alternatives.

A temporary presence, explained

A pop-up is a shop or display that opens for a limited time. It may be a way to introduce a product, sell a collection or hear directly from customers. A short visit to a market does not automatically represent demand across the whole city.

Reading the sample proposals

Each location page contains a fictional 14-day proposal, clearly labelled. The prices use Thai baht (THB). Rent and fit-out are separate from the refundable deposit. Dates, exact placement and customer data remain matters to confirm.

This guide provides location information and illustrative commercial examples. It does not establish legal requirements, arrange leases or provide legal, tax or investment advice. A real launch requires current checks with the venue and qualified local advisers.
